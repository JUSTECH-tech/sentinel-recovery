Why we exist

Fraud is common. Good support is not.

Most people who are scammed are left to piece together what happened across banks, payment apps, platforms, regulators and police — often while feeling embarrassed, exhausted and under pressure.

We built Sentinel Recovery to provide the thing that is usually missing: one calm, experienced point of contact who understands the system, knows what evidence matters, and will tell you the truth about what can and cannot be done.

We are not a law firm and we do not pretend to have a magic solution. We are a case-management and recovery firm that works with banks, exchanges, forensic specialists, regulators and legal partners to pursue legitimate routes.

Our values

What we hold ourselves to.

These are not slogans on a wall. They are the standards we use to decide which cases to take and how to treat every person who contacts us.

Honesty over hope

We would rather give you a difficult truth than a comforting promise we cannot keep. That includes telling you when recovery is unlikely.

Evidence over noise

We preserve the record, verify the details and build a timeline. Good evidence is what gives a recovery route a chance.

Dignity at every step

Fraud can happen to anyone. You will be treated with respect, without judgment, and at a pace you can manage.

Accountability

Clear scope, written fees, documented decisions and a complaints process. If we get something wrong, we want to know.

Standards

How we protect you.

Recovery work involves sensitive information and vulnerable people. These safeguards are non-negotiable.

  • No cold calls. We do not contact people out of the blue asking for money.
  • No upfront fee. The first assessment is free and no chargeable work starts without written agreement.
  • Data minimisation. We collect what the case needs, not everything we could ask for.
  • Conflict checks. We check for conflicts before accepting a case.
  • Partner due diligence. Legal and forensic partners are vetted before they receive any case information.
  • A complaints process. If something goes wrong, you can raise it formally and expect a response.
