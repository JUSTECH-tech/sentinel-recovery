Case studies

Four cases, four different outcomes.

Each one follows the same structure: what happened, what we found, what we did, and what actually came back.

Crypto fraud Reported 9 days after the final transfer

Fake trading platform emptied a retirement account

Loss reported$86,000
Assessment2 business days
OutcomePartial

The situation

A retiree had been guided through a “managed” crypto trading platform for four months. The dashboard showed steady gains, but every withdrawal request was met with a new fee or a tax demand.

What we found

The platform was not a registered exchange. Deposits had been converted to stablecoins and split across six wallets, two of which were still funded at a known exchange.

What we did

Preserved the dashboard, chat logs and bank records; submitted a law-enforcement referral and an exchange freeze request; and prepared a statement for the client's bank.

Outcome

About $19,000 was frozen before it moved and returned. The remainder had passed through mixing services and was not recoverable. We said so at the assessment stage rather than after months of billable work.

Business fraud Reported 36 hours after the payment

Invoice fraud at a family-run supplier

Loss reported£47,500
Recall window48 hours
OutcomeMajority

The situation

A supplier's finance inbox was compromised. A genuine invoice was replaced with near-identical bank details and paid late on a Friday afternoon.

What we found

The receiving account was a recently opened mule account at a second bank with a short transaction history. Part of the funds had already been forwarded onward.

What we did

Contacted both banks the same day, submitted a recall and indemnity request, coordinated with the company's insurer, and built a timeline for the police report.

Outcome

£18,000 was recalled before it left the mule account, and the insurer settled a further £11,000 — about 61% of the loss. The remainder had already been withdrawn.

Investment fraud Reported 14 months after the first payment

Unlicensed bond scheme that had already dissolved

Loss reported€120,000
Assessment3 business days
OutcomeNot recoverable

The situation

A couple had invested in a “guaranteed” corporate bond through an unlicensed adviser. Interest payments stopped, then the company disappeared.

What we found

The issuing company had been struck off eight months earlier. The directors had no traceable assets in the jurisdiction, and the funds had been spent rather than invested.

What we did

We declined the case. At no charge we prepared a structured evidence pack so the couple could file with the financial regulator and police, and referred them to a local victim support service.

Outcome

No money was recovered. We raise this case because declining an unwinnable case is part of doing the job properly — pursuing it would have cost the clients time and money for nothing.

Romance scam Reported 5 weeks after the first transfer

Payments split between card and bank transfer

Loss reported$18,400
Assessment1 business day
OutcomePartial

The situation

A client had sent money over five weeks to someone they had never met in person — part by card, part by bank transfer — after a fabricated medical emergency.

What we found

The card payments fell inside the chargeback window and the merchant had a record of disputes. The bank transfers had gone to an account that was already flagged.

What we did

Filed a chargeback on the card portion with a clear evidence bundle, notified the receiving bank, and supported a police report. One case manager handled all contact to avoid re-traumatising the client.

Outcome

$6,200 was returned through the card scheme. The transferred funds had been withdrawn and were not recovered. We closed with a written summary the client could give to their bank.

What they have in common

The pattern matters more than the scam type.

Across every case we take on, the same four factors decide what is realistically possible — and they are usually in your control.

  • Speed. The first 48 hours decide more outcomes than anything we do later.
  • Documentation. Banks and regulators act on original records and a clear timeline, not on recollection.
  • Realistic advice. We assess before we charge, and we decline cases that cannot be won.
  • No shortcuts. Nobody can “release” frozen funds for a fee. If you are told that, it is a second scam.
