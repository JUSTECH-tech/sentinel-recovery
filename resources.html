First 24 hours

Five steps that make a difference.

Do these in order if you can. If you are overwhelmed, do the first one and come back to the rest.

  1. 01

    Call your bank or payment provider

    Tell them you have been scammed and ask them to recall the payment, freeze the account or block the card. Note the time, the person you spoke to and any reference number.

    Do this first
  2. 02

    Stop all contact with the scammer

    Do not send more money, do not pay a "release fee" and do not let anyone remotely access your device. Block the account, but keep the messages as evidence.

  3. 03

    Preserve the evidence

    Take screenshots of profiles, chats, emails, websites and payment confirmations. Save original emails rather than forwarding them. Do not edit or delete anything yet.

  4. 04

    Report to the police and your fraud authority

    File a report with your local police and the relevant national fraud reporting service. Keep the reference numbers — they are useful for banks and recovery work.

  5. 05

    Secure your accounts

    Change passwords from a safe device, turn on two-factor authentication, check for unfamiliar logins and tell your bank if you reused any passwords.

Payment-specific steps

If you paid by…

Different payment methods have different recovery routes. These are the immediate steps worth taking.

Bank transfer

Ask your bank to recall the payment immediately. Provide the exact transaction reference, recipient name and account details. Ask whether a dispute or fraud claim is available.

Card payment

Contact your card issuer and ask about a chargeback or disputed transaction. Keep the order confirmation, listing and messages. Report the merchant to the card network if appropriate.

Cryptocurrency

Save the wallet addresses and transaction IDs. Report the platform or exchange involved. Do not pay anyone who promises to "trace" or "recover" your crypto for an upfront fee.

Gift cards

Contact the gift card provider immediately with the card numbers and receipts. Report it to the police. Gift card recovery is difficult, so speed is critical.

Evidence checklist

Keep the trail intact.

Good evidence is the difference between a vague complaint and a case that institutions can act on. Save originals where possible and keep a simple timeline.

  • Payment confirmations and bank or card statements
  • Original emails, with headers if possible
  • Chat messages, call logs and social media profiles
  • Website links, screenshots and company names
  • Wallet addresses and transaction IDs
  • Police, bank and regulator reference numbers

Do

  • Keep a simple timeline of what happened and when.
  • Save files in one folder with clear names.
  • Share only what is needed with people you trust.

Do not

  • Delete messages or close accounts before saving evidence.
  • Pay anyone who contacts you promising recovery.
  • Send passwords, PINs or one-time codes to anyone.
Recovery scams

Warning signs to take seriously.

People who have already been scammed are often targeted again. These are the patterns to watch for.

Guaranteed recovery

No legitimate service can guarantee a recovery. A guarantee is a sales tactic, not a legal promise.

Pressure to act now

Urgency is used to stop you checking the facts. Take your time and verify the company independently.

Payment in crypto or gift cards

No legitimate recovery firm asks to be paid in cryptocurrency, gift cards or wire transfers.

Requests for remote access

Anyone asking to install software or take control of your device is a serious risk. Say no.

Unsolicited contact

Be wary of anyone who contacts you first claiming to have found your lost money. Verify through official channels.

No written terms

A real firm will put its scope, fees and complaints process in writing before asking you to commit.
